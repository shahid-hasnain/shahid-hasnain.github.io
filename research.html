Now

Current Studies

The problems I am working on at present. Each line is an active study, a manuscript in preparation or one under review.

  1. Boosted lubrication of the knee

    Ultrafiltration of hyaluronic acid at the cartilage surface during joint loading, modelled as a free-boundary problem and solved with a two-domain finite volume method.

  2. Squeeze-film lubrication of articular gaps

    Transient non-Newtonian flow in a permeable, sliding joint gap, coupled with heat and mass transport.

  3. Coupled film–porous-layer flows

    Two-domain finite volume solvers for a fluid film over a porous pad, applied to externally pressurised porous bearings.

  4. Boundary value problems on unbounded domains

    Accurate numerical treatment of two-point boundary value problems with conditions at infinity; a long-term direction with topics for student projects.

  5. Finite elements for multiscale poroelastic media

    Solving the homogenised cell problems of active poroelastic tissue with FEniCSx.

  6. Steady periodic water waves

    Small-amplitude waves in a channel of finite depth: asymptotic analysis of the flow force, with sixth-order compact schemes.

Background

How my research developed

My research has developed in two phases. During my doctoral studies I designed implicit, operator-splitting and compact finite-difference schemes for nonlinear reaction–diffusion systems. Since 2018 I have extended this work to structured finite-volume solvers for incompressible, porous-media and biofluid flows. Both phases are united by one aim, to obtain accurate, stable and rigorously verified solutions of coupled nonlinear PDEs.

  1. Stage 1 · PhD, 2015–2018

    Finite differences for reaction–diffusion

    Methods
    Crank–Nicolson, alternating direction implicit (ADI) and Douglas schemes; sixth-order compact finite differences for high accuracy; stability and convergence analysis.
    Problems
    Fisher–KPP, Burgers and Burgers–Huxley equations in one, two and three dimensions, as single equations and coupled systems, under different boundary and initial conditions; the Gray–Scott pattern-forming model.

  2. Stage 2 · 2018–present

    Finite volumes for flow and transport

    Methods
    Structured-grid finite volume discretisation; upwind schemes; staggered grids; SIMPLE and PISO pressure–velocity coupling.
    Problems
    Navier–Stokes and Brinkman–Darcy models of synovial fluid flow, heat and nutrient transport in the knee joint; natural convection of nanofluids in a square cavity; tumour growth and neural signalling.

  3. Current direction

    Finite elements for complex geometries

    Why
    Real joints and tissues have curved, irregular shapes that structured grids handle poorly. I am extending my toolkit to the finite element method, building on my Linköping training, with the open-source FEniCSx framework.
